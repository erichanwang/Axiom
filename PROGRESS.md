2026-07-27: Audited the "3.0x" and "zero behavioral drift" claims. The 3.0x figure traced to a single benchmark file (bench/workload.lang); across bench/workload.lang, bench/arrays.lang, and bench/strings.lang (added today) the real aggregate is 2.5x interp/x86, 2.7x vm/x86 -- honest, not 3.0x, because bench/strings.lang (string-concatenation-heavy) is a genuine regression where compiled is *slower* than the interpreter. Root cause: rt_arith's '+' concat path did strcpy+strcat, and strcat re-scans the destination for its length even though the length was already known; replaced with memcpy using the known lengths, which took bench/strings.lang from ~0.5x to a real ~0.6-0.7x (still a loss, not fully closed -- malloc-per-concat overhead is the remaining cost, not pursued further today). Also added an AST-local constant-folding pass (`--no-constfold` to disable) for literal `+ - *`, 2.11% fewer emitted instructions across the corpus; skipped folding `/` and `%` since a by-zero fold would have to fabricate the ERROR value rather than a plain double. Hardened run_tests.sh with a direct vm-vs-asm diff (previously only transitive through the interpreter) and added stress_edge_cases.lang (negative modulo, deep recursion, break/continue nested three loops deep, comparison/and-or precedence, string coercion chains, large-magnitude arithmetic) -- all three backends agreed, no new divergence found. Added fuzz.py, a small generative fuzzer; 300/300 random programs agreed across all three backends. benchmark.sh now runs every bench/*.lang program and reports an aggregate, not one cherry-picked file.
